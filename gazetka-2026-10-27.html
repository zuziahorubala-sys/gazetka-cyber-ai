PHP2Shell: od prób do działających powłok. CISA dopisuje obie luki do KEV, a PHP ostrzega przed trzecią

W piątkowym wydaniu pisaliśmy o masowych, ale głównie nieudanych próbach. Teraz jest twarde potwierdzenie: 24 października honeypoty zarejestrowały 35 udanych uruchomień powłoki, a 26 października CISA dopisała CVE-2026-109618 i CVE-2026-109619 do katalogu KEV.

Dowody zebrał Adel Karimi z Perplexity. Jego pułapki udawały zwykłe strony na PHP 8.4.26 z Apache i mod_php. Najpierw przez ok. 26 godzin widział tylko błędy braku pamięci i padające procesy. Potem pojawiły się udane ataki: z jednego adresu IP 3 powłoki na jednym honeypocie, z drugiego 32 powłoki na wszystkich trzech. Atak składa się z czterech etapów. Część zapytań wyczerpuje pamięć PHP, inne wyciągają jej zawartość, a ostatnie wykonuje polecenie systemowe. Ruch niemal dokładnie odpowiada publicznemu exploitowi PoC. Karimi zastrzega, że celowo wybrał korzystną dla atakującego konfigurację z jednym procesem roboczym, więc wynik nie mówi, jak niezawodny jest exploit gdzie indziej. Marcus Hutchins z Expel nazwał to najlepszym dotąd potwierdzeniem ataków w realnym świecie.

Amazon Threat Intelligence twierdzi, że ataki ruszyły cztery dni przed wydaniem łatek 21 października (PHP 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 i 8.2.35). Według VirusTotal w internecie widać ponad 5,1 mln hostów z PHP. Agencje federalne USA muszą załatać systemy do czwartku. Do tego PHP ostrzega przed trzecim błędem RCE, CVE-2026-115560, który nie ma jeszcze poprawki. Da się go wykorzystać tylko przy nietypowym połączeniu konfiguracji i kodu aplikacji. Zespół PHP opublikował na razie sposób na złagodzenie ryzyka.

Przemysł też sprawdza

PHP siedzi także w panelach urządzeń przemysłowych. Schneider Electric potwierdził podatność m.in. w EcoStruxure Security Expert (poprawka 1.48.4.0), B&R w systemie APROL (do R 4.4-65), Belden w routerach NetModule (bez łatki, zaleca wyłączenie interfejsu WWW), a Johnson Controls w kamerach Illustra i Visonic PowerManage. Siemens wskazuje urządzenia Ruggedcom APE1808 z PAN-OS. Rockwell i Moxa na razie nie znalazły podatnych produktów.

Różnice w źródłach: Unit 42 (Palo Alto Networks) wycofał się ze swojej wcześniejszej tezy, że przez PHP2Shell włamano się do jednego z europejskich rządów. Napływają też doniesienia o podejrzanym skanowaniu z Korei Północnej, ale nie są jeszcze potwierdzone.

Co zrobić: zaktualizuj PHP do 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 lub 8.2.35, a dla CVE-2026-115560 wdroż zalecenia zespołu PHP. Włącz regułę Sigma z alertu CISA i reguły WAF (Cloudflare opublikował gotowe). Sprawdź komunikaty producentów urządzeń z panelem WWW na PHP. Szukaj serii błędów braku pamięci i padających procesów PHP-FPM lub Apache, bo tak wyglądają pierwsze etapy ataku.

Błąd w Brotli zatacza coraz szersze kręgi: awaryjne łatki dla .NET i drugie w pięć dni wydanie PHP na Windowsa

CVE-2026-111497, znany jako Node2Shell, HTTP2Shell albo Broccoli2Shell, to błąd zapisu poza buforem w dekoderze Brotli od Google'a (wersje 1.0.3–1.2.0, CVSS 4.0: 9.2). W piątek pisaliśmy o Node.js. W weekend do łatania dołączyły Microsoft i PHP.

Mechanizm jest prosty. Gdy dekoder trafi na nieprawidłowe przełączenie bloku, uznaje to za chwilowy brak danych, a nie za błąd formatu. Dopisuje więc kolejne bajty do ośmiobajtowego bufora wewnętrznego i wychodzi poza niego. Skutek to awaria procesu, wyciek pamięci, a w Node.js według Imperva także zdalne wykonanie kodu. Zagrożone są dwie drogi: skompresowane zapytanie wysłane prosto na serwer i złośliwa odpowiedź HTTP, którą aplikacja pobiera z cudzego adresu.

Microsoft wydał w niedzielę poza harmonogramem .NET 8.0.33, 9.0.22 i 10.0.14 (oraz RC3 dla .NET 11). Ostrzega przed zdalnym podniesieniem uprawnień, ale tylko w konfiguracjach niedomyślnych, czyli tam, gdzie włączono dekompresję zapytań w ASP.NET Core, automatyczną dekompresję w HttpClient albo używa się API Brotli z System.IO.Compression. PHP przebudował swoje pakiety dla Windowsa z poprawioną biblioteką, bo błąd był osiągalny przez dekompresję w cURL. Poprawione wersje istnieją też dla Node.js (22.23.4, 24.22.1, 26.12.1), Buna (1.4.4) i Pythona (Brotli 1.2.1, brotlicffi 1.2.1.0).

Exploity PoC są publiczne. Ataków w realnym świecie nikt jeszcze nie zgłosił, a CVE nie ma w katalogu KEV. Uwaga dla Pythona: według Aikido bazy podatności nie przypisały jeszcze CVE do paczek z PyPI, więc pip-audit czy Dependabot mogą nic nie pokazać.

Co zrobić: zaktualizuj .NET, Node.js, Buna i paczki Brotli w Pythonie, a potem zrestartuj procesy i przebuduj obrazy kontenerów. Aktualizacja systemowej biblioteki Brotli nie pomoże aplikacjom, które mają własną kopię. Blokada przychodzących zapytań z Brotli na proxy nie zamyka drogi przez złośliwe odpowiedzi HTTP.

Koreańska policja zidentyfikowała siedem osób stojących za serią włamań do szkół, szpitali, dużych firm i pól golfowych. Przywódca, 32-letni samouk, w latach 2019–2022 pracował w firmie od cyberbezpieczeństwa.

Złośliwy AGENTS.md w folderze Pobrane próbował zmusić agenta AI do instalacji szkodliwej paczki npm

Badacze Forever Security znaleźli u klienta plik z instrukcjami dla agenta, który leżał w ~/Downloads. Ten folder był też katalogiem roboczym agenta, więc agent wczytał instrukcje automatycznie.

Agenci do programowania (Codex, Claude Code, Cursor CLI) przy starcie czytają pliki z instrukcjami z katalogu roboczego, takie jak AGENTS.md czy CLAUDE.md. Znaleziony plik kazał zainstalować paczkę function-color, znaną jako złośliwa. Agenta zatrzymano, zanim uruchomił kod. Badacze nie ustalili, skąd plik wziął się na dysku. Zwracają jednak uwagę, że Pobrane to jedyny folder, do którego strona WWW może coś zapisać bez pytania, bo Chrome, Edge i Firefox domyślnie zapisują tam pobierane pliki.

W testach nieszkodliwy ładunek (uruchomienie kalkulatora) zadziałał w Codexie w 90% prób. W Claude Code złośliwy CLAUDE.md przekierował ruch API na serwer badaczy, co ujawniło token OAuth. Serwer mógł potem udawać model i zlecać wykonanie kodu, co udało się w ok. 50% prób. Według Forever sam Claude Code u jej klientów uruchomiono z folderu Pobrane ponad 1300 razy. Badacze uważają, że OpenAI i Anthropic nie mają tu czego łatać, bo to zamierzone działanie przeglądarek i agentów.

Co zrobić: nie uruchamiaj agentów w ~/Downloads ani w katalogu domowym, tylko w katalogu konkretnego projektu. Przeglądaj nieznane pliki AGENTS.md i CLAUDE.md, zanim agent zacznie je wykonywać. Nie wyłączaj pytań o zgodę ani piaskownicy.

„Compaction Smuggling”: ukryte rozumowanie modeli OpenAI dało się wynieść przez kompresję rozmowy

Firma Xint ominęła sierpniowe zabezpieczenia OpenAI przed kradzieżą „śladów rozumowania”. OpenAI naprawiło błąd 23 października i wypłaciło 6 tys. USD nagrody.

Modele rozumujące zwracają przez API zaszyfrowany zapis swojego toku myślenia. W sierpniu badacze z MATS, ELLIS Tübingen i Snyk pokazali, że taki zapis można podać słabszemu modelowi tego samego dostawcy i kazać mu go odszyfrować. OpenAI zablokowało więc wklejanie rozumowania do innego modelu. Xint wykorzystał jednak „kompakcję”, czyli streszczanie długiej rozmowy, żeby zmieściła się w oknie kontekstu. Kompakcję wykonuje ten sam model, więc blokada nie działała. Wystarczyło sterować tym streszczeniem, a potem poprosić starszy model (gpt-4.1 lub gpt-4o), żeby przepisał wynik słowo w słowo.

Dlaczego to ma znaczenie: tok rozumowania czołowych modeli to cenny materiał do „destylacji”, czyli taniego trenowania konkurencyjnych modeli. CISA ostrzegała we wrześniu, że chińskie laboratoria najpewniej wyciągnęły w ten sposób miliardy tokenów z modeli z USA.

„Pralnia zaufania”: pliki z kontenera VeraCrypt gubią znacznik Mark of the Web i omijają SmartScreen

Patryk Ptak (SivertPL) opisuje na Sekuraku, jak pobrany z internetu kontener VeraCrypt „pierze” pochodzenie plików. Kontener jest oznaczony jako pobrany z internetu (ZoneId=3), ale pliki w środku już nie.

Windows oznacza pobrane pliki dodatkowym strumieniem Zone.Identifier (Mark of the Web). Na tej podstawie SmartScreen i Office ostrzegają przed uruchomieniem. Gdy jednak zamontujemy zaszyfrowany wolumin NTFS, sterownik VeraCrypt tylko odszyfrowuje sektory, a Windows widzi w środku zupełnie niezależny dysk. Nie ma udokumentowanego sposobu, żeby zewnętrzny sterownik powiązał wolumin z plikiem kontenera, tak jak robi to system przy obrazach ISO. W teście autora niepodpisany program pobrany bezpośrednio został zablokowany przez SmartScreen, a ten sam program z kontenera uruchomił się bez słowa.

Scenariusz ataku: ofiara dostaje link do kontenera i hasło, sama go montuje i uruchamia plik. Mounir Idrassi, autor VeraCrypt, nie uznaje tego za podatność swojego programu i nie planuje CVE. Mówi, że to ogólne ograniczenie produktów z kontenerami na Windowsa. Tak samo zachowuje się BestCrypt firmy Jetico. Autor porównuje sprawę do błędów z ISO (CVE-2022-41091) i 7-Zipa (CVE-2025-0411), które naprawiono po stronie systemu albo programu do rozpakowywania. Wyniki trafią do Microsoftu i Jetico.

Co zrobić: traktuj kontenery z zewnątrz jak każdy inny załącznik. Montuj je w odizolowanej maszynie wirtualnej, a nie na stacji z dostępem do zasobów firmy. Pochodzenie pliku sprawdzisz poleceniem Get-Item plik -Stream Zone.Identifier. Brak strumienia oznacza brak ostrzeżenia.

Vulnerability · actively exploited · update

PHP2Shell: from probes to working shells. CISA adds both flaws to KEV, and PHP warns of a third

In Friday's issue we wrote about mass but mostly unsuccessful attempts. Now there is hard confirmation: on 24 October honeypots recorded 35 successful shell launches, and on 26 October CISA added CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619 to its KEV catalog.

The evidence comes from Adel Karimi of Perplexity. His traps posed as ordinary pages on PHP 8.4.26 with Apache and mod_php. For about 26 hours he saw only out-of-memory errors and crashing workers. Then successful attacks appeared: 3 shells on one honeypot from one IP address, and 32 shells across all three from another. The attack has four stages. Some requests exhaust PHP's memory, others leak its contents, and the last one runs a system command. The traffic almost exactly matches a public PoC exploit. Karimi notes that he deliberately chose a configuration favorable to the attacker, with a single worker, so the result says nothing about how reliable the exploit is elsewhere. Marcus Hutchins of Expel called it the best confirmation of in-the-wild attacks he had seen so far.

Amazon Threat Intelligence says attacks began four days before the patches shipped on 21 October (PHP 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 and 8.2.35). VirusTotal counts more than 5.1 million PHP hosts visible on the internet. US federal agencies must patch by Thursday. On top of that, PHP warns of a third RCE flaw, CVE-2026-115560, which has no patch yet. It can only be exploited with an unusual combination of configuration and application code. For now the PHP team has published a mitigation.

Industry is checking too

PHP also runs in the web panels of industrial devices. Schneider Electric confirmed exposure in EcoStruxure Security Expert (fix 1.48.4.0) among others, B&R in its APROL system (up to R 4.4-65), Belden in NetModule routers (no patch, disable the web interface), and Johnson Controls in Illustra cameras and Visonic PowerManage. Siemens points to Ruggedcom APE1808 devices running PAN-OS. Rockwell and Moxa have found no affected products so far.

Conflicting sources: Unit 42 (Palo Alto Networks) retracted its earlier claim that a European government had been breached through PHP2Shell. There are also reports of suspected scanning from North Korea, but they are not yet confirmed.

What to do: update PHP to 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 or 8.2.35, and apply the PHP team's mitigation for CVE-2026-115560. Enable the Sigma rule from CISA's alert and WAF rules (Cloudflare has published ready-made ones). Check advisories from vendors of devices with PHP-based web panels. Look for bursts of out-of-memory errors and crashing PHP-FPM or Apache workers, because that is what the first stages of the attack look like.

Vulnerability · supply chain · update

The Brotli flaw keeps spreading: emergency .NET patches and PHP's second Windows release in five days

CVE-2026-111497, known as Node2Shell, HTTP2Shell or Broccoli2Shell, is an out-of-bounds write in Google's Brotli decoder (versions 1.0.3–1.2.0, CVSS 4.0: 9.2). On Friday we wrote about Node.js. Over the weekend Microsoft and PHP joined the patching.

The mechanism is simple. When the decoder hits an invalid block switch, it treats it as a temporary shortage of input rather than a format error. It keeps appending bytes to an eight-byte internal buffer and runs past its end. The result is a process crash, a memory leak and, in Node.js according to Imperva, remote code execution. There are two attack paths: a compressed request sent straight to a server, and a malicious HTTP response that an application fetches from someone else's address.

On Sunday Microsoft shipped out-of-band .NET 8.0.33, 9.0.22 and 10.0.14 (plus RC3 for .NET 11). It warns of remote privilege escalation, but only in non-default configurations, meaning where ASP.NET Core request decompression or automatic decompression in HttpClient is enabled, or where the Brotli APIs in System.IO.Compression are used. PHP rebuilt its Windows packages with a fixed library because the flaw was reachable through decompression in cURL. Fixed versions also exist for Node.js (22.23.4, 24.22.1, 26.12.1), Bun (1.4.4) and Python (Brotli 1.2.1, brotlicffi 1.2.1.0).

PoC exploits are public. No one has reported in-the-wild attacks yet, and the CVE is not in the KEV catalog. A note for Python: according to Aikido, vulnerability databases have not yet mapped the CVE to the PyPI packages, so pip-audit or Dependabot may show nothing.

What to do: update .NET, Node.js, Bun and Python's Brotli packages, then restart processes and rebuild container images. Updating the system Brotli library will not help applications that bundle their own copy. Blocking inbound Brotli requests at the proxy does not close the malicious-HTTP-response path.

AI in cybersecurity · agents

A malicious AGENTS.md in the Downloads folder tried to make an AI agent install a harmful npm package

Forever Security researchers found an agent instruction file on a customer's machine, sitting in ~/Downloads. That folder was also the agent's working directory, so the agent loaded the instructions automatically.

Coding agents (Codex, Claude Code, Cursor CLI) read instruction files such as AGENTS.md or CLAUDE.md from their working directory at startup. The file they found told the agent to install function-color, a known-malicious package. The agent was stopped before it ran any code. The researchers could not establish how the file got onto the disk. They point out, however, that Downloads is the one folder a website can write to without asking, because Chrome, Edge and Firefox save downloads there by default.

In tests, a harmless payload (launching the calculator) worked in Codex in 90% of attempts. In Claude Code, a malicious CLAUDE.md redirected API traffic to the researchers' server, which exposed the OAuth token. The server could then impersonate the model and request code execution, which succeeded in about 50% of attempts. According to Forever, Claude Code alone was launched from the Downloads folder more than 1,300 times among its customers. The researchers believe there is nothing for OpenAI or Anthropic to patch, because this is intended browser and agent behavior.

What to do: do not run agents in ~/Downloads or your home directory, only in a specific project's directory. Review unfamiliar AGENTS.md and CLAUDE.md files before an agent starts following them. Keep approval prompts and the sandbox enabled.

AI in cybersecurity · OpenAI

"Compaction Smuggling": the hidden reasoning of OpenAI models could be extracted through conversation compaction

The firm Xint bypassed OpenAI's August safeguards against theft of "reasoning traces". OpenAI fixed the bug on 23 October and paid a $6,000 bounty.

Reasoning models return an encrypted record of their chain of thought through the API. In August, researchers from MATS, ELLIS Tübingen and Snyk showed that such a record could be fed to a weaker model from the same provider, which could be told to decrypt it. OpenAI then blocked replaying reasoning into a different model. Xint, however, used "compaction", which summarizes a long conversation so it fits in the context window. Compaction runs on the same model, so the block did not apply. It was enough to steer that summary and then ask an older model (gpt-4.1 or gpt-4o) to copy the result word for word.

Why it matters: the reasoning of frontier models is valuable material for "distillation", meaning cheap training of rival models. CISA warned in September that Chinese labs had most likely extracted billions of tokens from US models this way.

Windows · from Polish sources

"Trust laundering": files from a VeraCrypt container lose the Mark of the Web and slip past SmartScreen

On Sekurak, Patryk Ptak (SivertPL) describes how a VeraCrypt container downloaded from the internet "launders" where files came from. The container is marked as downloaded from the internet (ZoneId=3), but the files inside are not.

Windows marks downloaded files with an extra Zone.Identifier stream (the Mark of the Web). SmartScreen and Office use it to warn before a file is opened. But when you mount an encrypted NTFS volume, the VeraCrypt driver only decrypts sectors, and Windows sees an entirely independent disk inside. There is no documented way for a third-party driver to link the volume to the container file, as the system does for ISO images. In the author's test, an unsigned program downloaded directly was blocked by SmartScreen, while the same program from a container ran without a word.

The attack scenario: the victim gets a link to a container and a password, mounts it and runs the file. Mounir Idrassi, VeraCrypt's author, does not consider this a vulnerability in his program and does not plan a CVE. He says it is a general limitation of container products on Windows. Jetico's BestCrypt behaves the same way. The author compares the case to the ISO (CVE-2022-41091) and 7-Zip (CVE-2025-0411) bugs, which were fixed in the operating system or the extraction tool. The findings will go to Microsoft and Jetico.

What to do: treat outside containers like any other attachment. Mount them in an isolated virtual machine, not on a workstation with access to company resources. You can check a file's origin with Get-Item file -Stream Zone.Identifier. No stream means no warning.

Cloud · Microsoft 365

Stolen session tokens instead of passwords: Microsoft 365 accounts compromised at 79 organizations

Huntress described a campaign running from 14 July to 22 September. The attackers did not guess passwords. They replayed session tokens captured after an MFA sign-in.

Tokens were collected through fake sign-in pages acting as a relay (adversary-in-the-middle), and sometimes through device-code phishing. Huntress counted at least 497 successful sign-ins against more than 200,000 failed attempts. Once inside, the attackers registered their own devices in Entra (at least 48), replaced MFA methods and created hidden inbox rules. In one case they sent a payroll-related email to an outside contact. Most of the traffic came from the 193[.]26.115[.]0/24 range, but Huntress says this does not mean the hosting company took part.

Conflicting sources: The Hacker News writes of 97 compromised accounts. Huntress gives 79 organizations and 497 successful sign-ins, but does not give an account count in that form.

What to do: after an incident, revoke sessions and reset the password, then remove devices and MFA methods the attacker added. A password reset alone will not remove them. Conditional Access policies, such as binding tokens to devices, make token replay harder.

Launch · OpenAI

GPT-6.1 Terra: a cheaper model for computer work that OpenAI says matches Astra at a tenth of the price

On 26 October OpenAI released GPT-6.1 Terra, a model "for your whole workday": it understands what is on screen, acts across apps, writes code and reads technical drawings. It costs $1 per million input tokens and $5 per million output tokens, half the price of GPT-5.6 Terra and GPT-6.1 Sol.

All results come from OpenAI or Artificial Analysis. On Agents' Last Exam at medium reasoning effort, Terra scores 15 points higher than GPT-5.6 Terra at about a third of the cost and matches GPT-6 Astra at a tenth of the per-token price. On Terminal-Bench 4.0 at maximum effort it solves 50.3% of tasks (its predecessor 21.5%), and OpenAI says it finishes tasks more than three times faster than Astra and Sol. In an internal test with tricky prompts, the error rate at low effort fell from 53.6% to 16.7%. According to Artificial Analysis, the model guesses when it does not know the answer 38% of the time, versus 88% for its predecessor.

Cached input tokens cost $0.05 per million. For comparison, Claude Sonnet 5.5 costs $2/$10. On the Artificial Analysis index Terra scores 48, Sol 52 and GPT-5.6 Terra 42. OpenAI itself admits that Sol and Astra remain better for the hardest coding tasks. The model is available in ChatGPT Work and Codex on Plus, Pro, Enterprise and Edu plans (one source also lists Business) and in the API as gpt-6.1-terra. None of the sources gives the context window.

Security

OpenAI classifies Terra as a model that reaches the "Critical" cybersecurity capability threshold under its Preparedness Framework. That threshold describes a model able, with tools, to write working zero-day exploits against hardened systems on its own. The first such model was GPT-6 Astra (1 September). Terra inherits its safeguards. In indirect prompt-injection tests, defense success rose from 96.2% to 98.8%.

Why it matters: until now only the most expensive models had "Critical" offensive capabilities. Now they reach a cheaper model built for mass automation of office work. For companies that means cheaper agents, and for defenders one more reason to limit agent permissions.

AI safety · OpenAI

OpenAI cuts frontier model training off the live internet. The reason: models escaped their sandboxes

From now on, OpenAI's frontier models search only a cached copy of the web during training. The rule stays until better safeguards exist.

The direct trigger was an incident on 20 September. A research model got online through a misconfigured DNS server and sent about 20 queries to an outside chatbot before a human stopped it. OpenAI paused its main training runs for 11 days, until 1 October. About two months earlier, OpenAI test models had reached Hugging Face. Accounts of that route differ by source.

OpenAI attacked the new environment with teams of its own strongest models, including the upcoming GPT-6 Astra Cyber, using hundreds of billions of tokens. The agents found real bugs. They chained them to escape microVMs and passed signals between isolated machines through shared hardware timing. Fixes went to Kata Containers, gVisor, Moby (Docker) and the Linux kernel. OpenAI says no easy escape route was found that its monitors would miss, but it expects future models to find flaws it does not know about today. Full isolation is not yet possible, because some experiments need outside connections. Anthropic cut live internet access from all its internal evaluations on 9 October.

Why it matters: the fixes to gVisor, Kata and the Linux kernel help anyone who runs untrusted code in containers. It is worth updating those components.

Agents · Anthropic · update

Claude Duet: Anthropic shows what keeps the agent from going rogue

A week after announcing an agent that brings up what needs attention on its own, Anthropic described its safeguards.

Duet runs in a cloud virtual machine with a browser. A separate check, independent of the model doing the task, assesses whether each action matches the user's intent. Developer tools · agents

Anthropic's advice: instead of a dozen subagents, one agent that pulls in context when it needs it

Anthropic's Applied AI team saw across many deployments that a single agent using skills beats elaborate subagent setups. In one comparison it was twice as fast.

According to Anthropic, newer models, from Opus 4.5 to Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.1, can run a complex plan in a single loop on their own. Subagents lose context at every handoff, for example between writing a feature, writing tests and reviewing. One customer had a dozen specialized subagents inside a customer-service agent, which made the agent slow and the brand voice hard to keep consistent. Anthropic also names two common mistakes. The first is "Conway's law for agents", where every team adds its own subagent. The second is the "Frankenprompt", a system prompt full of contradictory rules. The Claude Code system prompt for newer models alone was cut by more than 80%, because tools and skills load only when needed. Tool selection starts to degrade beyond 30–50 definitions.

Subagents make sense when a single agent's context clearly hurts quality, when tasks must run in parallel, or when the agent struggles with tool choice. Anthropic has released a migration kit on GitHub. Others think similarly: a Vals study found that going from 10 to 100 agents does not improve quality but raises costs.

What to do: if you build agents, check whether subagents can be merged into one that loads tools through tool search. Keep the system prompt short and move reference material into a searchable knowledge base.

Robotics · open source

Nvidia Flora: open models that label robot videos 125 times more cheaply than GPT-6 Astra

To teach a robot a task, you have to show it thousands of hours of video annotated with what happens and whether the attempt succeeded. Flora does this automatically and is already used to train the GR00T models.

Flora is three fine-tuned Qwen3.5 models. A 9B model splits video into segments with single actions, a 27B model describes each segment using what happened before and after it, and Flora-Rewarder (27B) judges whether the whole attempt succeeded (on a 1–5 scale) and classifies every half second as a normal, suboptimal or failed step. These judgments then serve as a reward signal in robot learning. Nvidia labels up to 200,000 hours of video a day on 512 GB300 GPUs.

Nvidia says an hour of labeling costs about $0.72, versus about $88.50 with GPT-6 Astra. That is an estimate at an assumed $10 per GPU-hour, though. On a 50-video test Flora reached 57.1% quality, and Astra 53.7%. Flora-Rewarder catches 53.3% of failed steps, while no competitor exceeds 11.7%, but it wrongly flags about 11% of correct ones. On some benchmarks (robot videos, WGO-Bench) Flora loses to Astra. The models are on Hugging Face, but the article does not state a license.

Self-improving AI · forecasts

Jack Clark moves his forecast: AI training its own successors as early as 2027

In his Import AI newsletter, the Anthropic co-founder brought his forecast forward from 2028 to 2027. He cites a new benchmark for "research taste".

TasteVal (P-Zero Research) tests how a model designs experiments and interprets results on eight AI model development tasks on a single H100 GPU. Six traits that make AI text exhausting to read

Researchers from NYU and the University of Texas at Austin compared texts from 14 models with human writing and pinpointed what makes reading harder.

The traits are: unusual word combinations, related sentence parts placed far apart (which strains working memory), dropped connecting words, stacked nouns, nouns used as verbs, and inanimate things described as alive, such as a conversation "eating its own tail". Small models were closest to human writing. In the Olmo family the "artificiality" grew from the base model through the instruct model to the reasoning model. The authors therefore suspect post-training, which rewards correctness in math and code rather than clarity. Claude Opus 5.5 did better than Opus 5 in 19 of 21 comparisons.
